Mini Pita Pizzas

Lunch Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Let children choose one topping. Cut the cooled pizza to suit their eating skills.

Allergens: Wheat, milk

Ingredients & steps

What you need

  • 2 small pitas
  • 4 tbsp tomato sauce
  • ½ cup shredded mozzarella
  • Optional: 2 tbsp finely chopped, soft cooked vegetables

How to make it

  1. Heat oven to 400°F (200°C). Put pitas on a baking sheet.
  2. Spread each with 2 tbsp sauce and half the cheese. Add optional vegetables.
  3. Bake 8–10 minutes until the cheese melts and the center is hot. Cool, then cut into pieces suited to your child.

A little helper job: Let your child spread the sauce with a spoon. An adult handles the oven.

Creamy Cheeseburger Pasta Skillet

Dinner Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: A homemade Hamburger-Helper-style dinner: use small pasta shapes and a mild sauce. Keep extra cheese optional.

Allergens: Wheat, milk

Ingredients & Steps

What You Need

  • 1 lb ground beef
  • 2 cups dry elbow macaroni
  • 2½ cups low-sodium beef broth
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 cup shredded cheddar
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp garlic powder

How To Make It

  1. Brown the ground beef in a large deep skillet, breaking it into small pieces. Check that ground beef reaches 160°F; drain excess fat.
  2. Add broth, milk, pasta and seasonings. Bring to a gentle simmer, cover and cook about 10–15 minutes, stirring often, until pasta is tender. Add a little water if needed.
  3. Turn off the heat and stir in cheddar. Let it cool to a suitable eating temperature. Serve a familiar vegetable alongside.

Adult handles knives, heat and meat. Adapt food size and texture, supervise seated eating, and check labels and allergens. Times are estimates; these original recipes have not been kitchen-tested by Little Wins.

Mild Family Taco Night

Dinner Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Keep seasoning mild and offer meat, tortillas, and toppings separately. An adult prepares manageable pieces.

Allergens: Wheat in flour tortillas; milk if using cheese

Ingredients & Steps

What You Need

  • 1 lb ground beef
  • 8 small tortillas
  • 2 tsp mild taco seasoning
  • ¼ cup water
  • 1 cup shredded lettuce
  • 1 cup shredded cheddar
  • ½ cup salsa

How To Make It

  1. Cook ground beef in a skillet until it reaches 160°F, breaking it into small pieces. Drain excess fat.
  2. Add taco seasoning and water; simmer 3–5 minutes. Warm tortillas following package directions.
  3. Put toppings in separate bowls and let each person build a taco or keep familiar components separate. Cool and adapt pieces to each child.

Adult handles knives, heat and meat. Adapt food size and texture, supervise seated eating, and check labels and allergens. Times are estimates; these original recipes have not been kitchen-tested by Little Wins.

Slow-Cooker Mild Chicken Tacos

Dinner Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Choose mild salsa. Offer finely shredded chicken and a soft tortilla separately before mixing.

Allergens: Wheat in flour tortillas; check salsa labels

Ingredients & Steps

What You Need

  • 1½ lb fully thawed boneless chicken breast
  • 1 cup mild salsa
  • ½ cup low-sodium chicken broth
  • 8 small tortillas
  • Optional: shredded cheese or mashed avocado

How To Make It

  1. Place fully thawed chicken, salsa and broth in the slow cooker. Follow your appliance’s fill and liquid guidance; keep the lid closed while cooking.
  2. Cook on high about 3–4 hours or low about 6–7 hours, checking the manufacturer’s guidance. Time varies: every piece of chicken must reach 165°F on a food thermometer.
  3. Shred with two forks and mix into the cooking liquid. Serve in warmed tortillas or with rice, cooled and adapted for your child.

Adult handles knives, heat and meat. Adapt food size and texture, supervise seated eating, and check labels and allergens. Times are estimates; these original recipes have not been kitchen-tested by Little Wins.

Easy Slow-Cooker Bean Chili

Dinner Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Keep chili seasoning mild. Mash beans as needed and serve a familiar side alongside.

Allergens: Check broth and seasoning labels

Ingredients & Steps

What You Need

  • 2 cans (15 oz each) kidney or pinto beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can (15 oz) black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes
  • 1 cup low-sodium vegetable broth
  • 1 tsp mild chili powder
  • ½ tsp cumin

How To Make It

  1. Use canned beans, not uncooked dried kidney beans. Add all ingredients to the slow cooker and stir.
  2. Follow appliance guidance; cook about 3–4 hours on high or 6–7 hours on low until hot throughout. Times vary by cooker.
  3. Taste and adjust mild seasonings. Serve with rice, a baked potato, or bread. Cool before serving and adapt texture.

Adult handles knives, heat and meat. Adapt food size and texture, supervise seated eating, and check labels and allergens. Times are estimates; these original recipes have not been kitchen-tested by Little Wins.

Mix-And-Match Chicken & Rice Bowls

Dinner Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Set out chicken, rice, and soft vegetables separately. Let each child build a bowl or keep foods apart.

Allergens: Check rice, chicken and sauce labels

Ingredients & Steps

What You Need

  • 2 cups fully cooked shredded chicken
  • 2 pouches microwave rice (about 8.8 oz each)
  • 2 cups frozen vegetables
  • ¼ cup low-sodium broth
  • Optional: a familiar mild sauce

How To Make It

  1. Heat rice following package directions and cook frozen vegetables until tender.
  2. Warm fully cooked chicken with the broth until it reaches 165°F; do not rely on rice cooking time to reheat it.
  3. Serve components in bowls or separately with an optional familiar sauce. Cool and adapt sizes/textures before serving.

Pick-Your-Color Pasta

Dinner Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Serve pasta, vegetables, and cheese separately so everyone can choose what to combine.

Allergens: Wheat; milk if using cheese

Ingredients & steps

What you need

  • 1 cup dry small pasta
  • ½ cup frozen peas or finely chopped broccoli

  • Optional: 2 tbsp grated cheese

How to make it

Mini Banana Oat Pancakes

Breakfast Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Make small pancakes and serve fruit separately. Cut or soften the pancake as needed.

Allergens: Milk if using dairy; check oats for wheat cross-contact

Ingredients & steps

What you need

  • 1 ripe banana
  • ½ cup oat flour or finely ground oats
  • ⅓ cup milk or a familiar unsweetened alternative
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp oil for the pan

How to make it

  1. Mash the banana and mix with oats, milk, and baking powder. Let stand 3 minutes.
  2. Heat a lightly oiled skillet over medium-low heat. Spoon in small pancakes.
  3. Cook about 2–3 minutes per side until set throughout. Cool and cut to an appropriate size.

A little helper job: Your child can mash the banana. These are egg-free; do not add raw flour or batter as a topping.

Berry Yogurt Swirls

Breakfast Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Offer plain yogurt alongside the swirl. Mash or cut berries to suit your child.

Allergens: Milk if using dairy yogurt

Ingredients & steps

What you need

  • 1 cup plain yogurt or a familiar alternative
  • ½ cup soft berries, washed and mashed
  • Optional: 2 tbsp finely ground oats

How to make it

  1. Divide yogurt between two bowls.
  2. Mash the berries until no whole berries remain.
  3. Swirl berries into the yogurt and add optional ground oats. Serve seated, using a texture your child manages safely.

A little helper job: Make a swirl together with a spoon. No skewers, whole nuts, or hard toppings.

Cheesy Bean Quesadilla Triangles

Lunch Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Cut the cooled quesadilla into manageable pieces; serve a plain cheese portion if preferred.

Allergens: Wheat, milk

Ingredients & steps

What you need

  • 2 small flour tortillas
  • ½ cup rinsed canned beans, mashed
  • ½ cup shredded cheese
  • 1 tsp oil

How to make it

  1. Spread mashed beans and cheese over half of each tortilla and fold.
  2. Warm a lightly oiled pan over medium-low heat. Cook 2–3 minutes per side until heated through and cheese melts.
  3. Let cool and cut into soft pieces appropriate for your child.

A little helper job: Let your child sprinkle the cheese. An adult manages the hot pan.

Build-Your-Own Sweet Potato Bowls

Dinner Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Set out soft mash and familiar toppings separately. Let your child choose one addition.

Allergens: Milk if adding cheese or dairy yogurt

Ingredients & steps

What you need

  • 1 medium sweet potato, scrubbed
  • 2 tbsp plain yogurt or 1 tsp olive oil
  • Optional: 2 tbsp finely grated cheese

How to make it

  1. An adult pierces the potato several times with a fork. Microwave on a microwave-safe plate 5–8 minutes, turning halfway, until soft; time varies with size and microwave.
  2. Rest 2 minutes. An adult cuts it open carefully, scoops the flesh into a bowl, and mashes with yogurt or oil.
  3. Divide between bowls. Add optional cheese and cool well before serving.

A little helper job: Offer two familiar toppings and let your child choose. No need to hide ingredients.

Apple-Cinnamon Breakfast Oats

Breakfast Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Serve warm rather than hot, with a plain portion available. Soften the apple fully.

Allergens: Milk if using dairy; check oats for wheat cross-contact

Ingredients & Steps

What you need

  • ½ cup rolled oats
  • 1 cup milk or water
  • ½ peeled apple, finely grated
  • ¼ tsp cinnamon

How to make it

  1. Combine oats and liquid in a small saucepan. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring, for 5–7 minutes or according to the package.
  2. Add grated apple and cinnamon; cook 2 minutes until the apple is soft. Add a little water if needed.
  3. Cool well and divide into two bowls. Adjust texture to your child.

A little helper job: Measure oats into a cold bowl.

Soft Rainbow Egg Scramble

Breakfast Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Keep a plain scramble portion and offer soft vegetables separately.

Allergens: Egg; milk if using cheese

Ingredients & Steps

What you need

  • 2 eggs
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped, soft cooked vegetables
  • 1 tsp oil
  • Optional: 2 tbsp shredded cheese

How to make it

  1. Beat eggs in a bowl. An adult heats oil in a nonstick pan over medium-low heat.
  2. Add eggs and cooked vegetables. Stir gently until the eggs are firm throughout with no liquid remaining.
  3. Add optional cheese, remove from heat, and cool. Cut into manageable pieces.

A little helper job: Choose which already-cooked vegetable to add.

Creamy Bean Toast Fingers

Lunch Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Offer the spread and toast separately or spread a thin layer. Adjust bread texture and pieces to your child.

Allergens: Wheat; check bread labels

Ingredients & Steps

What you need

  • ½ cup rinsed canned white beans
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • 1–2 tbsp water
  • 2 slices familiar bread

How to make it

A little helper job: Mash cooled beans with a fork under supervision.

Warm Pear Yogurt Cups

Breakfast Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Serve softened pear and plain yogurt separately if mixed textures are hard.

Allergens: Milk if using dairy yogurt

Ingredients & Steps

What you need

  • 1 ripe pear, peeled and finely diced
  • 2 tbsp water
  • 1 cup plain yogurt or familiar alternative
  • Optional: pinch of cinnamon

How to make it

  1. An adult simmers pear with water in a covered saucepan over low heat for 5–8 minutes until very soft.
  2. Mash the pear and let it cool. Add optional cinnamon.
  3. Divide yogurt into bowls and spoon pear alongside or swirl it through. Serve a texture your child handles safely.

A little helper job: Swirl cooled pear into yogurt.

Mix-And-Match Baked Potato Bowls

Dinner Idea

Make It Kid-Friendly: Offer a soft potato base and two familiar toppings for a choose-your-own dinner.

Allergens: Milk if adding yogurt or cheese

Ingredients & Steps

What you need

  • 2 small potatoes, scrubbed
  • ½ cup rinsed canned beans, mashed
  • 2 tbsp plain yogurt or 1 tsp oil
  • Optional: 2 tbsp finely grated cheese

How to make it

  1. An adult pierces potatoes several times and microwaves on a microwave-safe plate for 8–12 minutes, turning halfway, until soft; timing varies.
  2. Heat mashed beans in a saucepan until hot throughout. Let potatoes rest 2 minutes.
  3. An adult carefully opens potatoes and mashes the flesh with yogurt or oil. Serve beans alongside or spoon them over the potato; add optional cheese. Cool thoroughly.

A little helper job: Choose which familiar topping to serve alongside.
