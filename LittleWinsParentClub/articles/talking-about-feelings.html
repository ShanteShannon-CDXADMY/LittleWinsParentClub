Children’s Mental Health

How do I talk with my child about their feelings?

A low-pressure conversation that leaves room for different ways of communicating.

October 9, 2026

Quick Answer

A low-pressure conversation that leaves room for different ways of communicating.

Jump To The Practical Steps

Use This IdeaResources

Choose connection over an interview

Try a brief check-in while drawing, walking, or sitting together. Ask whether they want you to listen, help, or simply stay nearby. You do not need a complete explanation at once. Younger children might choose a picture; an older child may prefer writing or a later conversation. Respect a comfortable form of communication.

Reflect what you hear

Use an observation rather than a label: “It sounds like lunchtime was difficult.” Check whether you understood. Avoid telling your child what they must feel or treating every emotion as a diagnosis. Your response can hold both care and limits: “Being angry makes sense; I’ll help keep everyone safe.” If you misunderstand, make a simple repair and ask again.

Return to the conversation

Ask what support would help tomorrow and agree on one realistic action. Keep school or health details private rather than posting them in the Parent Club. CDC’s child mental health resources provide a broader starting point for learning about wellbeing and support. If concerns persist or daily functioning changes, discuss them with a qualified professional instead of relying only on family check-ins.
