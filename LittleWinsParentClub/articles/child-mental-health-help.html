Children’s Mental Health

When should I seek mental health support for my child?

Move from a concern to a practical appointment plan.

October 9, 2026

Quick Answer

Move from a concern to a practical appointment plan.

Jump To The Practical Steps

Use This IdeaResources

Consider impact, duration, and safety

NIMH advises considering professional help when emotions or behavior persist, cause distress, or interfere with life at home, school, or with friends. You do not have to identify a diagnosis first. A qualified professional can assess what is happening. If behavior is unsafe or your child talks about harming themselves or others, seek help immediately.

Prepare for the first conversation

Write a few everyday examples, when you first noticed them, and what has changed. Include strengths and your child’s own account. Start with their healthcare professional and ask about referral to someone experienced with children. Ask what information from school is useful, what support is available while waiting, and how to arrange a follow-up.

Keep a clear support path

Save the contact name, appointment date, and next action somewhere private. Ask providers how they involve caregivers and accommodate communication needs. The Beautiful Minds resource page offers official information and support directories; the Parent Club is peer conversation, not clinical care. In the United States, call or text 988 for crisis support. For immediate danger or a life-threatening emergency, call 911.
