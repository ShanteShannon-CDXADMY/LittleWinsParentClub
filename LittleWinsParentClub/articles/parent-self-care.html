Parent Self-Care

A little care before the day. A little peace after it.

You care for everyone else. Make a small place for yourself in the routine, too.

Little Wins Parenting Companion · Published October 4, 2026 · 3 minute read

Quick Answer

Try about ten minutes in the morning and at night, split into smaller moments if that fits better. Keep what helps. Skip what adds pressure.

Jump To The Practical Steps

Use This IdeaResources

Morning: a gentle start

  1. Water and something to eat. Have a drink and an easy breakfast when you can. A perfect meal is not the goal.
  2. Freshen up for yourself. Wash your face, brush your teeth, and get into comfortable clothes. Use your usual simple skincare.
  3. Take one quiet minute. Breathe naturally, notice how you feel, or look out of the window before jumping into the next task.
  4. Choose one priority. Ask, “What would make today feel a little easier?” Pick something realistic, and give a less urgent task permission to wait.
  5. Make room for support. Ask for something specific, such as help with breakfast or a few minutes to shower. If help is unavailable, choose a pause that keeps your children safely cared for.

Night: set the day down

  1. Do the basics. Wash up, brush your teeth, and follow your usual simple skincare. You do not need a shelf of products.
  2. Prepare one thing, then stop. Set out clothes, fill a bag, or choose breakfast. The whole house does not need to be finished.
  3. Name one little win. Write a sentence about something you handled with care. You can also jot down tomorrow’s worry so you do not have to keep rehearsing it.
  4. Give yourself a screen break. Put your phone aside for a short wind-down when possible, while staying reachable for caregiving needs.
  5. Pick a quiet comfort. Read a page, listen to gentle music, stretch comfortably, or simply rest. Make it something you enjoy.

The two-minute version

Morning: a sip of water, a quick wash, and one kind thought: “I can take this day one step at a time.”

Night: brush your teeth, name a little win, and pause. If you are exhausted, rest can be the routine.

When the day refuses to cooperate

These steps do not have to happen before the kids wake up or after everyone goes to sleep. Move them to the first workable moment. An interrupted routine still counts. Shared care and practical help matter, too; self-care is not a way to do every job alone.

Parent Mental Health & Support

Small routines can support you, but you can also ask for professional care. If distress persists or affects daily life, contact a qualified clinician. These U.S. resources offer starting points; use local services if you live elsewhere.

  • NIMH: Caring for Your Mental Health — practical information and when to seek support.
  • NIMH: Find Help — finding qualified mental health care.
  • SAMHSA National Helpline — treatment referral information.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline — call or text 988 in the U.S. for immediate emotional or suicide-crisis support. For immediate danger, call emergency services.
